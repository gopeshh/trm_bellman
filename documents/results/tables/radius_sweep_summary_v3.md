## Purpose

Demonstrate that Model B's stability advantage comes from **contraction enforcement** (L_z < 1), not from **projection clipping** (R=10 causes 100% saturation). By increasing R until saturation → 0%, we isolate the contraction effect.

## Key Findings

### 1. Contraction provides stability INDEPENDENT of projection

At R=100 (0% saturation, no clipping), Model B still achieves:
- **2x lower Δ_z** (20.10 vs 39.29)
- **39% higher argmax agreement** (91% vs 52%)

This is purely from contraction enforcement (L_z < 1).

### 2. Projection amplifies stability at small R

At R=10 (100% saturation), Model B achieves:
- **4x lower Δ_z** (1.36 vs 5.43)
- **4% higher argmax agreement** (98% vs 94%)

The combination of contraction + projection provides the strongest stability.

### 3. Projection without contraction is insufficient

Model A at R=10 (with projection clipping) still shows 5.43 Δ_z, which is 4x worse than Model B at the same R. Clipping alone doesn't provide the stability that contraction does.

## Interpretation for Paper

**Claim**: "Contraction enforcement (L_z < 1) provides depth-invariant stability"

**Evidence**:
- At R=100 where saturation=0% and projection has no effect, Model B (contraction) shows 2x lower Δ_z than Model A (no contraction)
- The stability advantage persists across all radius values
- Contraction is the primary mechanism; projection is an additional safety net
